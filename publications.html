Evaluating Waste-Based Stabilizers: Review on Mechanical and Durability Performance for CSEB Production

ICCEI 2025, RUET, Dec 12-14, 2025

The increasing environmental footprint of Fired Clay Brick (FCB) manufacturing has led to interest in Compressed Stabilized Earth Blocks (CSEB) as an eco-friendly option. CSEB manufacturing, however, is restricted by scalability due to the requirement of clay-rich subsoil. In this review, the efficacy of using waste-based stabilizers in improving the mechanical and durability properties of CSEBs is assessed. 27 peer-reviewed papers (2000-2025) concerning four leading groups of waste i.e., agricultural waste, industrial waste, Construction and demolition (C&D) waste, and plant-based natural fibers and binders, were examined. Compressive strength, flexural strength, and water absorption were utilized to analyze the performance. Industrial waste provides maximum mechanical strength, while C&D waste provides strength and durability in a comparative way. Natural fibers show great flexural capacity, though water resistance is mediocre. A performance map based on radar identifies Recycled Aggregates using 8% cement and silica fume-glass powder combinations as the optimum stabilizers. The study concludes that the selection of material can consider soil compatibility and local waste availability. The research works for the benefit of sustainable construction by promoting the circular use of waste and optimizing efficient stabilizer selection for the production of CSEBs.

Keywords: CSEB; Waste-based Stabilizer; Recycled Additives.

Shear Strength Properties of MICP-Treated Sand Reinforced with Discrete Nylon Fibers

Under review, Geosynthetics Conference 2027, Feb 2027

Microbially induced carbonate precipitation (MICP) has gained increasing attention as a sustainable ground-improvement technique for sandy soils because it enhances particle bonding through calcium carbonate precipitation. However, bio-cemented sands often exhibit brittle behavior after treatment, which may limit their practical application in geotechnical and erosion-control systems. Fiber reinforcement offers a potential means of improving the mechanical response of MICP-treated soils by providing tensile resistance and crack-bridging capacity. However, research on nylon fiber reinforcement in MICP-treated sandy soil remains limited. This study evaluates the combined effect of MICP and randomly distributed nylon fiber on the strength behavior of poorly graded sand collected from the riverbed of Gazaria, Munshiganj, Bangladesh. The soil consisted of 97.62% sand and 2.38% fines and was classified as poorly graded sand according to the Unified Soil Classification System. MICP treatment was performed through a biostimulation approach, and nylon fibers were incorporated at 0%, 0.25%, 0.5%, 0.75%, and 1.0% by dry weight of soil. Treated and untreated specimens were evaluated using unconfined compressive strength tests, direct shear tests, and microstructural characterization. The untreated sand had no meaningful unconfined compressive resistance, whereas MICP bio-cementation increased the UCS to 270 kPa. With 0.5% nylon fiber reinforcement, the UCS further increased to 401 kPa, accompanied by improved ductility. Direct shear results indicated that MICP primarily improved apparent cohesion through carbonate bonding, while nylon fiber reinforcement enhanced frictional resistance and deformation capacity. Microstructural observations confirmed dense calcium carbonate bonding between sand particles, and FTIR analysis identified carbonate-related peaks near 1410 cm⁻¹ and 872 cm⁻¹, supporting calcite formation. The findings suggest that nylon fiber reinforcement can complement MICP by improving the strength and ductility of bio-cemented sand, although durability, constructability, cost, sustainability, and field-scale performance require further evaluation.

Spatiotemporal Variation of Water Quality Parameters in Hatirjheel Lake, Dhaka, Bangladesh: A Data Synthesis Approach

Under review, ICACE-2026, Dec 2026

Hatirjheel was developed as a water-based urban system; however, it has failed to serve its intended purpose due to severe water pollution. There are some research papers about Hatirjheel, but they fail to present the full scenario and lack seasonal and spatial analysis. This study combines all seasonal values of different parameters: temperature, pH, apparent color, true color, turbidity, total suspended solids, total dissolved solids, total solids, electrical conductivity, empirical factor, carbon dioxide, carbon dioxide acidity, p-alkalinity, total alkalinity, total hardness, carbonate hardness, chloride, and total iron. The data were collected from different published papers and laboratory tests. Seasonal and spatial variations were observed. From the results, it is obvious that the water is more polluted in the rainy season because inflow and runoff water exceed the treatment capacity. In the dry season, wastewater flows through different pipes, which keeps the lake water quality better. Overall, the findings compare and combine the information and make it easier to understand the change in water quality on a spatial and temporal basis. These findings will help in monitoring and managing the lake in the future.

Impact of Urbanization on Local Hydrology: A Computational Approach for Stormwater Risk Assessment in Dhanmondi, Dhaka

Poster, NSF NHERI GSC 4th International Mini-Conference, May 2026

Rapid urbanization increases impervious surfaces such as roads and buildings, reducing infiltration and increasing stormwater runoff. In cities like Dhaka, this leads to frequent urban flooding during heavy rainfall events. This project develops a computational urban flood prediction system to estimate runoff, flood depth, and drainage performance using hydrologic and hydraulic modeling techniques using MATLAB. The system also provides a user-friendly GUI for rapid flood risk assessment and urban drainage planning. Key findings: increasing impervious area significantly increases runoff; existing drainage pipe capacity may become insufficient during intense rainfall; flood depth increases rapidly under high rainfall intensity; pipe diameter upgrades reduce overflow risk. Urbanization significantly increases stormwater runoff and urban flood risk. The developed computational framework successfully predicts runoff, flood depth, and drainage performance, providing a simple and efficient tool for urban flood risk assessment and drainage planning.

Solar Roadways: Paving the Way to Sustainable Energy

Poster, Professional Practices and Communication Sessional, BUET, Feb 2027

The Solar Roadway means structurally-engineered solar panels along the road surface. The methodology includes direct solar panels on roads and bifacial PV modules. Aligned with global renewable energy and transportation objectives, solar roadways offer accident prevention features. In Bangladesh, they hold potential for significant electricity generation, promoting solar energy use and sustainability. Despite costs, solar roadways represent a promising era in power generation and transportation, offering free energy with climate change mitigation.

Modern Architecture from Civil Engineering Perspective

Poster, Architectural, Engineering and Planning Appreciation Sessional, BUET, Feb 2023

Modern Architecture from a Civil Engineering Perspective explores the evolution, principles, and practical implications of modern architecture with a focus on civil engineering. The study highlights key architectural movements, influential architects, and iconic structures, emphasizing functionality, innovation in materials, structural efficiency, and sustainability. It also examines the challenges and successes of implementing modern architecture in Bangladesh, including issues related to urbanization, material use, and energy efficiency. The work underscores the role of civil engineers in shaping sustainable, adaptable, and socially responsive built environments.

Slope Stability Analysis and Erosion Control Using Vetiver Grass: A Green Approach

Magazine article, Yearly Magazine of ASCE Student Chapter, BUET, Jun 2024

Slope stability is a critical concern in various engineering and environmental applications, as unstable slopes can lead to landslides, erosion, and other detrimental effects. Traditional methods of slope stabilization often involve the use of rigid structures and synthetic materials, which can have negative environmental impacts. In recent years, there has been growing interest in sustainable and eco-friendly approaches to slope stability analysis and erosion control. One such approach gaining attention is the utilization of Vetiver grass (Chrysopogon zizanioides), a versatile and robust plant with remarkable soil binding and erosion control properties. This article explores the concept of slope stability analysis using Vetiver grass and highlights its benefits, challenges, and potential applications. Slope stability analysis involves assessing the potential for soil mass movement and designing appropriate measures to prevent landslides and erosion. Conventional methods often involve engineering structures such as retaining walls and geotextiles. However, these methods can be expensive, resource-intensive, and may disrupt the natural ecosystem. Vetiver grass offers an alternative solution by harnessing the natural strength of vegetation to stabilize slopes and control erosion.
